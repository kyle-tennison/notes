Technology Hill

Every product slides down the hill over its life. While it is new, its cost is mostly R&D, engineering and profit, so it gets made where the innovation happens. Once it becomes a commodity, its cost is mostly fabrication, so production moves to wherever making things is cheapest.

The x-axis runs backwards in time

The right edge is high technology: a brand-new product nobody else can make yet. The Time Axis arrow points left. As competitors catch up, the product moves left through the four stages and ends as a post-competitive commodity.

The shaded wedges are cost shares

On the right, R&D, engineering and profit take most of the price, and margins are fat because you have no rivals. On the left, those wedges pinch to almost nothing and fabrication is nearly the whole cost.

The curves are what you compete on

Technology matters enormously for new products and fades to nothing. Marketing and management (branding, distribution, lean operations) take over once everyone can make the same thing.

That decides location

When fabrication dominates cost, the cheapest producer wins, so production leaves for developing regions with lower labor cost. When R&D and engineering dominate, the work stays in developed regions with the skilled engineers and research base.

The slide's bottom line: "Manufacturing production hollows out, while manufacturing innovation sets in." Developed countries keep losing production of mature products, so they have to keep climbing the hill by innovating new ones.
