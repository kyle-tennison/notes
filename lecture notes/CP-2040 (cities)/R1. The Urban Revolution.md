# R1. The Urban Revolution

By Gordon Childe

---

The idea that civilizations progress:
Savage (Hunter Gatherer) $\to$ Barbarian (Crude but Neolithic) $\to$ Civilization (Living in cities, etc.)

Cities in the Old World can be defined as post-Neolitic settlement, occurring around the advent of the Bronze age. 

It was 5000 years ago in the valleys around the Nile when irrigation made enough of a food surplus. For the first time, people could become specialists in something; they weren't forced to be laborers. It was here by the Nile that the first cities really formed.

But to develop a definition off a "city" based on this is flawed because the Mayans, totally separate from this, also had their own cities.

The author instead gives 10 "rather abstract criteria" that define a city:
1. A settlement more extensive and populated than any previous settlement. 
2. Had a class of people who were not strictly making food—a specialty class.
3. The surplus from farmers was taxed
4. Monumental public buildings distinguish cities (?)
5. A ruling class that handled planning and orginization, even if corrupt.
6. Compelled to create systems of recording and sciences. Writing is thus a common mark itself for civilization.
7. The invention of writing, calendars, etc.
8. Artwork
9. Sometimes traded foreignly 
10. Craftsmen were provided with raw material for their craft, and "paid" (through food) for it.
