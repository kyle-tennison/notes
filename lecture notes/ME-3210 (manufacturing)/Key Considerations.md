# Key Considerations

### Operation Levels
- **Manufacturing Enterprise:** The business side of things. A group of value-adding activities to facilitate production. This includes sales, finance, marketing, product design, fab, logistics, R&D, administration. 
- **Manufacturing System:** The systems that actually go into manufacturing: process analysis, production scheduling, QA, shop floor configuration.
- **Manufacturing Process:** The steps taken to create and assemble parts.
- **Manufacturing Engineering:** Optimizing the manufacturing process via engineering methods.

### Evolution of Manufacturing 

Industry 1.0: The first industrial revolution towards the end of the 1800s.

Industry 2.0: After WWII when machines got more complex and solid state electronics debuted. 

Industry 3.0: PLC (Programmable Logic Controller), Robotics, AGVs (Automated Guided Vehicle). 

Industry 4:0: IoT and cloud integration

Industry 5.0(?): Simulation, learning, prediction, etc.

### 1. Global Markets and International Consumption

Supply chains have gone global; nowadays a single part can be composed of constituent parts from across the globe.

![](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322270152/figure/fig4/AS:631610022055940@1527598972239/Boeings-global-supply-chain-Source-The-Boeing-Company.png)

Different markets have different labor costs, and many companies exploit this for cheaper production.

**FDI** stands for foreign-direct investment. You compare two candidate sites, A and B, and pick the one with the lower "penalty."

$$P(A) = \alpha L^a_A+\beta D^b_{a-M}W$$
- $P(A)$ is the penalty of locating at $A$
- $L_A$ is the labor cost at $A$ (units?)
- $D_{A-M}$ is the geographic distance from $A$ to the market
- $W$ is the product size
- $\alpha, \beta, a, b$ are weighting parameters
